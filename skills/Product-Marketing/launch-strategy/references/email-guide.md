# Email Guide for Product Launches

Imgix launch emails are short, scannable, and benefit-first. Follow the Imgix brand voice for all copy.

## Email Types

### Customer Announcement Email
Sent to existing Imgix customers when a feature launches.

**Subject line:** Short, specific, benefit-led.
- Good: "New: Turn any image into video with one URL"
- Good: "Your images just got faster. Here's what changed"
- Bad: "Important update regarding your Imgix account"
- Bad: "YOU WON'T BELIEVE What We Just Launched!!!"

**Body structure (5-step):**
1. **Confident opening paragraph:** 1-2 sentences stating what shipped and why it matters. Tone is forward-looking and assured, not breathless or flat. Don't start with "We're excited" (too personal for a brand email) or "This is an update about" (too dry). Start with the capability and its value.
2. **Bold lead-in feature list:** Use bold feature names with one-line benefit descriptions. Bullets, not paragraphs. Each line should make the reader think "I want that."
3. **Forward-looking close:** 1-2 sentences that point ahead. What's coming next, or what this unlocks for the reader. Creates momentum, not a dead end.
4. **One clear CTA:** "See what's new" or "Try it now" (with UTM parameters). Single button or link. Don't split attention.
5. **Team sign-off:** "The Imgix Team" (capital T). Not a specific person's name for launch announcements. Individual names are for 1:1 emails, not product announcements.

**Total length:** 5-8 sentences. The reader should scan the entire email in under 30 seconds. If it feels too short, it's probably right.

## Tone Calibration for Launch Emails

Launch emails have a specific tone that's different from nurture emails or 1:1 outreach. Get it wrong and the email either reads like a status update (too flat) or a personal note from a friend (too warm for a brand announcement).

**The right tone: confident and forward-looking.**
Think of a company you respect announcing something they're proud of. It's not giddy. It's not corporate. It's assured. "Here's what we built, here's why it matters to you, here's what's next."

**Too flat (status update):**
> "We've released several updates to Imgix Video. These include adaptive streaming, closed captions, and super resolution. You can read more in the docs."

**Too warm (personal note):**
> "I'm so thrilled to share what we've been working on! This has been months in the making and I can't wait for you to try it."

**Right tone (confident, forward-looking):**
> "Imgix Video just got a major upgrade. You can now deliver adaptive streams, auto-generate captions in 100+ languages, and upscale video to 4K, all from the URL. Here's what's new."

**Key principles:**
- First person plural ("we") is fine. First person singular ("I") is not, unless the email is genuinely from one person (like a founder letter).
- Excitement is expressed through the strength of the capabilities, not through adjectives or exclamation points.
- The sign-off is "The Imgix Team," not an individual, unless there's a specific reason for a personal touch.
- Forward-looking language ("here's what this unlocks," "and we're just getting started") is better than backward-looking ("we've been working hard on this").

### Prospect/Nurture Email
Sent to prospects or leads who aren't yet customers.

**Subject line:** Lead with the problem the feature solves or the outcome it enables.
- Good: "Stop managing five tools for one visual workflow"
- Good: "Video optimization without the infrastructure headache"

**Body structure:**
1. One sentence: the pain point or opportunity
2. 1-2 sentences: how Imgix addresses it (tied to the new feature)
3. One CTA: "Start your free trial" or "See how it works" (with UTM parameters)

**Total length:** Even shorter than customer emails. 2-4 sentences.

## General Email Rules

- One primary CTA per email. Don't dilute with multiple asks.
- Paragraphs: 1-2 sentences for mobile readability
- No filler adverbs, no em dash overuse
- All CTA links must include UTMs: `utm_source=email`, `utm_medium=email`
- Campaign: `utm_campaign=q[quarter]_[year]_[feature]_launch`
- Content tag: `utm_content=cta_primary` or `utm_content=cta_secondary`

## A/B Testing

For major launches, draft 2 subject line variants:
- Variant A: Benefit-led (what the reader gets)
- Variant B: Feature-led (what's new)

Example:
- A: "Optimize and deliver video with the same simplicity as images"
- B: "New: Imgix Video now supports HLS, DASH, and closed captions"
