# Content Format Guide

Channel-specific guidance for applying the Imgix voice across different content types.

## Blog Posts

**Structure:**
- Open with a clear, benefit-driven statement about what's new or what the reader will learn
- Use the problem → solution pattern for each major section
- Close with a clear next step (free trial, guide link, webinar). All CTAs must include UTM parameters.
- 3-minute read target for announcements; 5-7 minutes for thought leadership

**Opening pattern (from real Imgix posts):**
> "Our latest release expands Imgix Video with smarter tools for managing, transforming, and creating video content."

> "Starting today, every Imgix customer gets full access to our complete visual media platform."

Notice: no throat-clearing, no "we're excited to announce," no buildup. Just what it is and why it matters.

**Section pattern:**
Each feature or topic section follows this rhythm:
1. What it does (one sentence, benefit-framed)
2. The problem it solves (1-2 sentences acknowledging the reader's pain)
3. How Imgix solves it (2-3 sentences with specifics)
4. What this means for the reader (1 sentence connecting back to their workflow)

**Closing pattern:**
Warm, direct, and action-oriented. Examples:
> "New to Imgix? Perfect timing. Start your free trial and start exploring what's possible."

> "If you're already using Imgix, you'll automatically get access to these features when your current subscription transitions."

## Email Campaigns

**Subject lines:** Short, specific, benefit-led. No clickbait, no ALL CAPS, no excessive punctuation.
- Good: "Your images just got faster. Here's what changed"
- Good: "New: Turn any image into video with one URL"
- Bad: "YOU WON'T BELIEVE What We Just Launched!!!"
- Bad: "Important update regarding your Imgix account"

**Body:**
- Keep emails SHORT. The reader should be able to scan the entire email in under 30 seconds.
- Lead with the single most important thing the reader needs to know
- Include 1-2 clear value messages: what's the "why" for the reader?
- One primary CTA per email. Don't dilute with multiple asks.
- Keep paragraphs to 1-2 sentences for mobile readability
- Total body copy: aim for 3-5 sentences max. Seriously, shorter than you think. If it feels too short, it's probably right.
- Sign off warmly but briefly: "The Imgix team" or a specific person's name
- All CTA links must include UTM parameters with `utm_source=email` and `utm_medium=email`

**Nurture sequences:**
- Each email should deliver standalone value with a clear "why should I care" message
- Progress from awareness (what Imgix does) to consideration (how it helps your specific use case) to action (try it / talk to us)
- Space emails 3-5 days apart in most sequences
- Shorter emails perform better. Resist the urge to over-explain.

## Social Media

**Tone:** Slightly more energetic than blog posts, but still Imgix. No forced hashtag stuffing, no emoji overload. One emoji per post is fine when it adds clarity.

**LinkedIn:**
- Write like a person sharing something interesting, not a brand pushing a product. If it reads like ad copy, rewrite it.
- Lead with an insight, observation, or data point. Not a pitch.
- Good: "For every second of loading time saved, conversion rates increase by 10%. Here's how teams are getting those seconds back →"
- Bad: "Product images are your best sales tool, but they're static. Video content drives 80% higher engagement..." (this sounds like a landing page, not a LinkedIn post)
- Keep it to 3-5 sentences total. Say the one thing that matters and link out for the rest.
- Sound like a smart person at the company sharing something they find genuinely interesting, not a marketing team broadcasting a message.
- Do NOT use the staccato fragment pattern ("No X. No Y. No Z."). It's the most obvious AI tell on LinkedIn.
- All links must include UTMs with `utm_source=linkedin` and `utm_medium=organic_social`

**X/Twitter:**
- Tight, punchy, benefit-first. Link to longer content for the full story.
- Good: "New: Auto-generate video previews from any video with a single URL parameter. No transcoding required."

## Landing Pages

**Headline:** One clear benefit statement. Should make sense without any other context.
- Good: "The platform for your visual media"
- Good: "Optimize, transform, and deliver images at scale"

**Subheadline:** Adds specificity to the headline. Who it's for and what they get.

**Body sections:** Use the problem → solution pattern. Each section addresses one pain point and one Imgix capability. Keep copy scannable. Bullets for feature lists, short paragraphs for narrative sections.

**CTA buttons:** Action-oriented, specific. All buttons must link to URLs with UTM parameters.
- Good: "Start free trial" / "See it in action" / "Talk to our team"
- Bad: "Learn more" / "Click here" / "Submit"

## Product Announcements / Release Notes

**Framing:** Lead with the category of improvement, then the specifics. Don't bury the value under technical details.

**Structure (from real Imgix releases):**
1. One-paragraph summary of the release theme
2. One-paragraph context on why this matters / the unified platform story
3. Individual feature sections (using the standard problem → solution pattern)
4. Closing that ties it together and points to next steps

**Feature naming:** Use descriptive names that communicate function. "Smart Cropping" not "AutoCrop v2." "Video Previews" not "ClipGen."

## Case Studies

**Structure:**
1. Customer context (who they are, what they do)
2. The challenge (specific, quantified where possible)
3. The solution (how Imgix helped, with technical specifics)
4. The results (metrics, quotes, business impact)

**Voice:** Let the customer's story lead. Imgix is the enabler, not the hero. Use direct quotes from the customer whenever possible. Keep Imgix commentary to framing and context.
